**Project:** [jazz-standards-analytics](https://github.com/mbergr/jazz-standards-analytics) — end-to-end pipeline over the jazz standards canon (ingest → model → visualize).
**Status:** Ingestion + staging + marts + metrics/docs complete. Dashboard not started.

## Done

- `dbt docs generate` + lineage: the full DAG (sources → staging → intermediate → marts → metrics) is auto-derived from `ref()` / `source()`.
- Three metric models (`rpt_`) over the star schema:
  - `rpt_most_recorded_standards` — recordings and distinct artists per standard.
  - `rpt_standard_longevity` — first/latest recording year, span, and jazz eras spanned.
  - `rpt_composer_footprint` — standards, recordings, and composition-year range per composer.
- Tests: `not_null` / `unique` on the report keys. `dbt build` → PASS=43, 0 errors (11 models, 32 tests).

## Findings

- **Longevity:** "Ain't Misbehavin'" and "I Got Rhythm" lead with a ~98-year recording span. "Honeysuckle Rose" spans the most jazz eras (5).
- **Composer footprint:** George Gershwin dominates (11 standards, 55 recordings), then Duke Ellington (6 / 43).

## Decisions

- **Metrics as `rpt_` marts** — one layer, materialized as tables, not a separate project.
- **Docs generated locally** — the dbt docs site lives in `target/` (gitignored). Hosting it (GitHub Pages) is a later polish.

## Data caveats

- Longevity uses Spotify `release_year`, not recording date. Reissues stretch the span, and the earliest "recording" year can even precede the composition year (e.g. I Got Rhythm shows 1920 < 1930). Release-era, not recording-era.
- "Most recorded" is still capped at 10 by the ingestion limit.
